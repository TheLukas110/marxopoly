# IP review: Common Ground working title

Reviewed 12 September 2026. This records engineering changes and unresolved issues;
it is not a legal opinion, clearance search, or a guarantee against litigation.

## What changed

- Replaced the public “Marxopoly” branding with the working title “Common Ground”.
  The package scope, browser storage keys, Git repository name and historical
  copyright notice remain for compatibility and attribution; they are not the
  public product name. The new title has **not** been cleared for trademark use.
- Replaced the square perimeter board in every playable view: 3D worlds now use
  circular routes with equal spaces; 2D uses a numbered district route with a
  separate control panel. Replaced property colour strips with small markers,
  changed district colours, and replaced the red hotel model with owner-coloured
  upgrade markers. Regenerated the preview images from the world geometry.
- Replaced the property names, purchase prices, rent ladders, build costs, taxes,
  depot tolls and works multipliers. These are new gameplay values, not a legal
  threshold or proof that the game is sufficiently different.
- Wrote two new twelve-card decks about the fictional districts, with new text
  and a different mix of effects. Removed “GO” and “GO TO HOLD” skin labels.
- Removed the unsupported claim that the game was not derived from any commercial
  board game. Added a short non-affiliation notice. A disclaimer is not a defence
  by itself, and free distribution or an MIT licence does not settle third-party rights.

## What still requires review

The game still has forty spaces, eight property groups, buying/rent, auctions,
mortgages, buildings, two dice, doubles, a holding area and event decks. The order
of tile kinds and several core rules remain similar to Monopoly. Changing names,
values or geometry does not automatically eliminate copyright, trademark,
design-right or unfair-competition claims about the overall product.

Before choosing the release name, obtain a proper clearance search covering the
territories and goods/services you will offer. Check DPMA, EUIPO and WIPO records,
similar names and unregistered uses; “Common Ground” is only a working title.
Have an IP lawyer review the actual game, visuals and distribution plans, including
the remaining combination of mechanics and tile sequence. That may lead to further
changes or a licence; this code change cannot promise that no rights holder will sue.

The five scene previews are generated by the repository's own mesh renderer
(`packages/client/scripts/generate-world-previews.mjs`). This review did not establish
the provenance of every historical code contribution or perform a full asset,
dependency-licence, patent or registered-design clearance. Preserve attribution and
check rights to any new imagery, fonts, names or user-supplied card text before release.

The operator notice still contains sample details. Replace these with the actual
provider information and assess applicable privacy/disclosure obligations for
Pages, the chosen backend host, browser storage, chat and server logs. These details
depend on the operator and deployment; they cannot be invented by this patch.

## Legal sources and limits

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes game ideas and methods from protectable
literary/artistic expression such as rules text and board artwork. That distinction
does not clear a particular implementation and is not a conclusion about German
or EU law. [Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)

Trademark confusion can arise from similar sound, appearance, meaning or overall
commercial impression; identical names are not necessary.
[USPTO: Likelihood of confusion](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion)

For Germany, DPMA recommends researching earlier rights and checking EUIPO/WIPO
in addition to its own register. Register searches alone also do not cover every
earlier sign used in trade.
[DPMA: Trademark searches](https://www.dpma.de/english/trade_marks/trade_mark_search/)

German unfair-competition law addresses imitation under specified circumstances,
including avoidable confusion about commercial origin and exploitation/impairment
of a competitor's reputation. This needs a fact-specific assessment.
[§ 4(3) UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__4.html)

Provider information requirements are separate from game IP clearance.
[§ 5 DDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html)
